## Part 2 — What the site cannot fix (the real work)

### 1. The video is the product. Everything else is packaging.

Yaron's core point. A booker at the price level worth chasing decides in the
first 20 seconds of the sizzle reel. Nothing on the page compensates for
footage that looks like a phone on a table.

**The minimum viable pro reel:**
- Pick **one** already-booked show with a good-sized crowd (60+) and a decent room.
- Hire **two operators**: one on a gimbal roaming for audience faces, one locked
  off wide on a tripod. Two angles is the entire difference between "raw" and
  "produced".
- Get **audio off the mixing desk**, not the camera mic. This is the single
  cheapest upgrade and the most noticeable.
- Brief the shooters that the money shots are **reactions, not the magic** —
  gasps, hands over mouths, clapping, people turning to each other.
- Cut to **60–75 seconds**, fast, with music. Yaron's ₪30,000 video is a
  ceiling, not a floor; ₪3,000–5,000 for a two-camera night plus a freelance
  editor gets 80% of the way.

**Until that exists:** re-cut what already exists. The current trailer is the
strongest asset on the page — it should be the only long-form video above the
fold, and the close-up trick clips should probably eventually be deleted rather
than merely collapsed. A magician who posts nine phone videos of card moves
reads as a hobbyist; one who posts three clips of a room losing its mind reads
as an act.

### 2. Get the review count up, deliberately

22 is a weak number and Yaron is right to say so. It is also the easiest number
on this list to fix, because the shows already happened.

- Go back through `shows.csv` — roughly 40 events, ~2,300 people. Every one of
  those hosts is a review that was never asked for.
- Ask **at the event**, not a week later: "if you enjoyed it, a Google review
  genuinely helps me — I'll send you the link tonight." Then send it that night.
- Build the ask into the booking flow permanently: confirmation email → show →
  thank-you message with the review link within 12 hours.
- Realistic target: 22 → 60+ within a few months of consistent asking. At 60+,
  the number becomes an asset worth displaying again — at which point the
  "Performed for" bar can gain a **"60+ five-star Google reviews"** line.

### 3. Price like the act you want to be

Higher-end bookings are lost at the quote stage more often than the website
stage.

- Stop quoting a single number. Offer **three tiers** (e.g. 45-min show / show +
  60-min walkaround / full evening with pre-show mingling). Buyers who are given
  one price compare it to zero; buyers given three pick the middle one.
- Never quote below the price at which the show is worth doing. A cheap quote
  from a "professional mentalist" is itself evidence of inexperience — it is the
  same signal Yaron read off the stats bar.
- Quote in writing, same day, on letterhead, with the invoice terms stated. The
  new How It Works section promises this — it has to be true.

### 4. Go where the high-end money actually is

Home parties and sheva berachos will not fund the video. The buyers with
budgets are:
- **Hotels and event venues** — Olive Tree is already on the roster; that is a
  reference, use it. Waldorf, Mamilla, Brown, Inbal, David Citadel.
- **Corporate HR and event agencies** — the leads lists in this repo
  (`event_planner_leads.txt`, `event_companies_leads.txt`) are already built and
  largely unworked.
- **Anglo community organisations and fundraisers** — they book English-language
  entertainment at real budgets and talk to each other.

One booked hotel gig funds the two-camera shoot, which unlocks the next five.
That is the loop to close.

### 5. Press

There are eight drafted press releases in this repo (`press_jpost.txt`,
`press_toi.txt`, `press_arutz7.txt`, and others) about the volunteer IDF shows,
and no record that any were sent or picked up. A single real byline in the
Jerusalem Post or Times of Israel would justify an "As featured in" line that
does more for perceived seniority than any stat. Send them.
