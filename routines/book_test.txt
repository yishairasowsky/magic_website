BOOK TEST

PREMISE:
Researchers in cognitive science claim that when a person concentrates deeply on a single word, something measurable happens. Not just in their brain, but in the space around them. I am not reading minds. I am reading something more subtle than that. Some call it the afterglow of a focused thought.

PEOPLE:
- Book Owner: provides the book, opens it at the end to confirm
- Page Stopper: briefly comes up, stops the riffle, returns to seat immediately
- Line Picker: seated, shouts a line number
- Word Picker: seated, shouts a word number

METHOD:
1. Page Stopper comes up to stage left. Performer riffles pages toward them. They say stop. Performer glimpses the facing page and adds 1. That is the page.
2. Page Stopper is sent straight back to their seat. "Remember your page -- don't tell anyone."
3. Line Picker shouts a line number. While counting aloud ("line one, line two..."), performer secretly opens to the page and holds thumb at the chosen line.
4. Word Picker shouts a word number. While counting aloud ("the first word, the second..."), performer reads the target word and locks it in.
5. Performer now knows the word. Book is closed and returned to Book Owner.
6. Performer asks the room: "Think of random letters -- five or six. When I say now, call them out one at a time, slowly."
7. Performer fakes writing the letters called out, but actually writes an anagram of the target word. If a letter that happens to be in the word is called (e.g. "E"), acknowledge it: "Who said E? Good..."
8. Performer shows what is "written." "Can you make a word out of these letters?"
9. Book Owner opens the book to the page and confirms the word.

ANAGRAM CONSTRUCTION:
Keep the first and last letters in rough position, scramble the middle. The result looks like a genuine scramble but is solvable.
Example: THINKING -> TNHIIKNG

SUBTLETY: Deliberately write one letter "wrong" while constructing. Someone in the room will call it out -- "but there's a K!" -- which (a) feels spontaneous, (b) confirms a real letter in the word is present, and (c) pulls the audience into solving it. Correct it visibly and thank them.

PATTER:

PREMISE:
"Researchers in cognitive science claim that when a person concentrates on a single word, something happens -- not just in the brain, but in the space around them. Some call it the afterglow of a focused thought. I want to see if I can read it."

PAGE:
[Hand book to Book Owner. Call Page Stopper up.]
"Just watch the pages as I riffle. Say stop whenever you feel like it."
[Riffle toward them. Peek on stop.]
"That page -- remember it. Don't tell a soul. You can sit down."

LINE:
"Any page in a book has dozens of lines."
[Secretly open to the page. Begin counting lines visibly.]
"Line one, line two, line three... you -- pick any number you like."
[Line Picker calls a number. Count to it. Hold thumb there silently.]
"Line [N]. Got it."

WORD:
"And each line has about ten words."
[Count along the line with your eyes.]
"The first word, the second, third... which would you like?"
[Word Picker calls a number. Read that word. Lock it in. Close the book.]
"[N]. Perfect."
[Return the closed book to Book Owner.]
"Please hold this -- keep it closed for now."

ANAGRAM:
"I want everyone to think of a random letter. Don't say it yet -- just hold one in your mind. When I say now, call them out one at a time, slowly."
[Say "now." Begin faking to write the called letters. Write the anagram.]
"Can you make a word out of these letters?"
[Pause. Let the room try.]
"Open the book. Page [N], line [N], word [N]."
[Book Owner opens and reads the word aloud. The anagram resolves.]

TRANSITION:
"Thank you."
[Beat.]
"That was a word from a book -- something random on a page nobody chose. Now something more personal."

REMINDERS:
- Never say "prediction" -- it is a feeling, a residue, an afterglow
- Send Page Stopper back to seat the moment they stop the riffle
- The anagram reveal is the climax -- hold the silence before "open the book"
- Don't explain after it lands. Let the room sit with it.
