BOOK TEST
(Ben Cohen revision, 2026-08-25)

PREMISE (under 1 min):
There is a documented phenomenon -- researchers in cognitive science claim that
when a person concentrates deeply on a single word, something measurable happens.
Not just in their brain -- in the space around them.
You are not reading minds. You are reading something more subtle than that.
A residue. A trace left by focused thought.

PEOPLE:
- One person seated (Page Stopper) -- stops the flip
- Book Owner -- hands book, finds the word at the end
These can be the same person. Keep it to two people max, both seated.

METHOD (peek):
Walk to Page Stopper with book. Fan/flip pages toward them.
They say stop. You glimpse the facing page and add 1.
That is their number.

STAGE NOTES:
- No one comes on stage. You go to them.
- Page Stopper and Book Owner can be the same person.
- Line number and word number: call out to the room, anyone shouts one.
- Book returns to Book Owner to find the word. They stay seated.
- You never touch the book again after the flip.

PATTER OUTLINE (bullet points):

SETUP:
- "There's a study out of [vague institution] -- they found that when a person
  holds a single word in their mind, something lingers in the air around them.
  Not for long. But long enough."
- "I want to try something with that. All I need is a book -- any book."
  [Book Owner hands it over]

PAGE:
- Walk to someone. "Just say stop whenever you feel like it."
  Flip pages toward them -- peek on the stop.
- "That page. Remember it." [Close book, hand back to Book Owner]

LINE + WORD:
- "Somebody -- line number, shout one out." [Anyone calls it]
- "And a word number." [Anyone calls it]

FIND IT:
- "Page [N]. Line [N]. Word [N]. Take your time -- find it.
  Don't say it. Just hold it in your mind."
- [Pause. Let them find it. Fill with silence or one quiet line:
  "This is the part they say is impossible."]

REVEAL:
- Reach into pocket. Produce folded paper or card, face down.
- "I'm going to tell you something. What's on this isn't a prediction.
  It's a feeling I had -- a notion I wrote down before we even started.
  Say the word."
- [They say it. Turn the card around. Hold the silence.]

SEND BACK / TRANSITION:
- No applause cue. Just: "Thank you."
- Beat. Then: "That was a word from a book -- something random, on a page
  nobody planned. Now I want to try something more personal."

DEAD MOMENT FILLERS (while they search for the word):
- "Most people assume this kind of thing requires a trick. It might.
  Or it might not. That's what makes it interesting."
- [Silence is also fine here -- let the room feel the wait]

REMINDERS:
- Never say "prediction" -- always "a feeling" / "a notion" / "something I sensed"
- Don't dwell after the reveal. If they react, let it happen. Move on.
- The premise does the work. Don't explain the effect after it lands.
