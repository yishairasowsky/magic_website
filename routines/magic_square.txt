MAGIC SQUARE
============

POSITION IN SHOW: Opener

PRE-SHOW:
Before the show, approach one guest and have them write a number on a billet.
Use Marsh Peek to read it. Remember the number.
That guest is your plant for the opener -- they do not know they are.

OPENER FRAMING:
"This show is going to be interactive -- I need everyone's help from the start.
So before we begin, I want all of you to think of a number. Any number up to 100.
If you already have one in mind, raise your hand."
[Pause. Hands go up. Gently make eye contact with your pre-show guest -- a slight look or nod is enough to prompt them to raise their hand if they haven't already.]
"You -- what's your number?"
[They call it. You already know it. Begin the square immediately, no hesitation.]

---

Some of you have heard of the numbers square.

Someone calls out a number, and the performer constructs a grid of smaller numbers that all add up to that total. The smallest possible square is for the number 15. Watch: 3-8-4, 9-5-1, 6-7-2. Every row adds up to 15. But that's nothing.

When we do it, we won't take a few minutes. We'll take seconds.

And we won't use three rows -- we'll use four.

And we won't use a small number like 15 or 35. We'll use anything up to 100.

[to a spectator, ideally the host or father of the family]

Sir -- please don't say anything yet, but I want you to think of a number. Not an easy low number like 33. Something high, and ideally something random rather than a round number. Have it in your mind?

I am not going to take minutes. The moment you give me the number, watch -- I start writing immediately.

Call it out.

[he calls his number -- immediately begin filling the square]

 8  11   .   1
 .   2   7  12
 3   .   9   6
10   5   4   .

[turn the pad and walk through it]

First row: 11 and 1 is 12, plus 8 is 20, plus [N-20] -- that's [N].

And the same is true for every row.

[pause for applause, then:]

But that's not all. Because you chose such a great number, I wanted you to have the columns as well. Left column: 8 and 3 is 11, plus 10 is 21, plus [N-21] -- equals [N].

[pause]

And the diagonals. This one: 8 and 2 is 10, plus 9 is 19, plus [N-19] -- that's [N]. And the other: 1 and 7 is 8, plus 10 is 18, plus [N-18] -- also [N].

[pause]

And each of the four quadrants adds up to [N]. The four corners -- [N]. The four center squares -- [N]. These two and these two, wherever you slice it -- [N].

Wherever you add them, you get [N].

[pause, then to room]

Numbers are supposed to be predictable. That's the whole point of mathematics. And yet somehow that felt like anything but.

A big round of applause for [SPEC'S NAME].
